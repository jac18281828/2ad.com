This book follows two thousand years of war. It begins with the first armies of Mesopotamia and
Egypt and ends with the fall of the Western Roman Empire. Carey's combined-arms system follows heavy and
light infantry, heavy and light cavalry, and how each army learned to use them together.

### Raphia, 217 BCE

Antiochus III came to the Seleucid throne in 233 BCE. He "inherited little more than the
capital of Antioch" (*Warfare in the Ancient World*, p. 154). Parthia had taken the east, and
decades of war with Ptolemaic Egypt had worn down the west. To win back his inheritance, he
marched south from Syria and met Ptolemy IV at Raphia, on the frontier of Palestine and Egypt.

Antiochus brought over 62,000 infantry, 6,000 cavalry and 102 Indian war elephants raised in
Bactria. Ptolemy answered with over 45,000 infantry, 4,000 cavalry and some seventy-three
African elephants. Both kings put their phalanx in the centre and their elephants in front of
the wings.

Antiochus's elephants drove back the smaller African elephants on his right, and his cavalry
chased Ptolemy's guard from the field.

> **Raphia shows what elephants can and cannot do.**
