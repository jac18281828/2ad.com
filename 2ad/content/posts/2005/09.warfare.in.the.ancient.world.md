Published 30 September 2005.

The book follows two thousand years of war, from the first armies of Mesopotamia and Egypt to
the fall of the Western Roman Empire. Brian's thread is the combined-arms system: heavy and light
infantry, heavy and light cavalry, and how each army learned to use them together. Chariots at
Megiddo, the Greek phalanx, Alexander's Macedonian army and the Roman legion each take their
turn, and each battle comes with multi-phase tactical maps.

Reviewers found it dense but rewarding. The World History Encyclopedia praised the depth on arms,
armour and tactics, and HistoryOfWar.org called it a good starting point for the serious reader.

The globe shows the world after Alexander, when his generals had split his empire among them:
the Antigonids in Macedon, the Seleucids from Syria to Bactria, and the Ptolemies in Egypt.
Antioch was the Seleucid capital. At Raphia in 217 BCE, the two great successor kingdoms met
in one of the largest battles of the age.

Brian was my history professor in college. When he needed a regional map illustrator, I had
been doing computer cartography at MapQuest, LandVoyage and Rand McNally. We built each regional
map from a GMT projection, then finished it in Illustrator. I drew this globe new for this
post, with GMT 6 and ETOPO 2022 from my [bedrock](https://github.com/jac18281828/bedrock)
image.
