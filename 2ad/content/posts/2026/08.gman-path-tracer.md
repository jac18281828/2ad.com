## Try it

```sh
curl -sL https://github.com/jac18281828/gman/releases/download/1.0.0/gman-1.0.0-linux-x86_64.tar.gz | tar xz
curl -sLO https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jac18281828/gman/1.0.0/samples/vase.rib
export PATH="$PWD/gman-1.0.0-linux-x86_64/bin:$PATH"
```

Add one line to `vase.rib`, under `PixelSamples 2 2`:

```
Option "pathtracer" "integer samples" [64]
```

```sh
gman -r gmanpathtracer vase.rib
```

That writes the picture above in about three minutes on one core of an Apple M3 Max; the ray tracer takes seconds. Linux arm64 and macOS arm64 builds are on the [releases page](https://github.com/jac18281828/gman/releases).

## One integrator

[John: keep, cut or reword.]

Soft shadows, glossy reflection, refraction and diffuse interreflection each used to be a feature of its own. A path tracer gets all of them from one loop: follow a path from the camera, sample the surface's scattering at each hit, and add the light that reaches it.

- **Every shader returns a BSDF.** Lambert of `Cs` by default; a GGX microfacet lobe beside the diffuse one for `plastic`, `paintedplastic`, `metal` and `shinymetal`; exact-Fresnel delta lobes for `mirror` and `glass`.
- **Next-event estimation.** Every vertex casts a shadow ray toward a light chosen in proportion to its power over distance squared.
- **Area lights.** `AreaLightSource "arealight"` turns the `Sphere` or `Disk` after it into an emitter, sampled uniformly by area.
- **Multiple importance sampling.** Shadow rays and BSDF bounces that reach the same area light are weighted by Veach's power heuristic, so a sharp glossy lobe under a large light converges without fireflies.
- **Russian roulette** ends a path from its third vertex.

It renders on one thread, so far.

## What changed underneath

[John: a paragraph at most, or cut.]

1.0 breaks the plugin ABI. Shaders were singletons that returned a reference to a function-local `static`, so two threads shading at once would tear each other's colour. Each `Surface` now constructs its own shader instance, `computeCi` and `computeOi` are `const` and return colour by value, and every out-of-tree shader rebuilds. The framebuffer holds unclamped colour until the output driver narrows it, so a bright sample survives to `Exposure`.
