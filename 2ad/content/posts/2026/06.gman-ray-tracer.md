Ray tracing renders a picture by firing rays from the eye into a scene and asking what each one hits. Shadows, mirrors and glass all come from that one move.

A z-buffer renderer, the kind most games use, shades each surface from its lights alone. It never asks what stands between a surface and its light, so nothing casts a shadow.

## One ray per pixel

A ray tracer follows light from the eye to the lamp. Almost none of the light leaving a lamp reaches your eye, and a light path is the same in either direction, so the renderer starts where the answer is needed. There is one ray from the eye through each pixel, and it looks for the first surface it hits.

## What a ray hits

A ray is an arrow with a start point O and a direction D. The point at parameter t is O + tD. To find what it hits, ask each object how far along the ray it sits, and keep the smallest distance.

For a sphere of center C and radius R, a point P is on the surface when |P − C|² = R². Put P = O + tD into that and the condition becomes a quadratic in t:

<div class="highlight"><pre><code>(D·D) t² + 2 D·(O − C) t + |O − C|² − R² = 0</code></pre></div>

The roots are the hits. The smaller positive one is the first surface the ray meets; a ray that misses has no roots. Every primitive, from the polygon to the torus, has its own intersection test.

## Shadows, mirrors and glass

From each nearest hit, the ray tracer fires a shadow ray toward each light. If the ray reaches the light, the light adds its intensity to the point; if something opaque is in the way, the light adds nothing and the point is in shadow.

A reflective surface fires another ray in the reflected direction. gman's `mirror` shader is C++, but in RenderMan's shading language it is one line:

```
Ci = Os * Cs * Kr * trace(P, R);
```

`R` is the reflected direction and `trace()` fires a ray along it. Whatever that ray finds, the mirror returns scaled by `Kr`. If it finds another mirror, that one fires a ray of its own.

![the robot's dome as a mirror, showing the room]({static}/images/2026/gman-mirror.png)

Glass sends light both ways: part reflects and the rest bends through the surface, so one hit fires two rays. Face-on, glass reflects about 4 percent of the light; at a grazing angle, nearly all of it, which is why the edges of a glass vase look like mirrors.

![the vase in glass, the room bent through it]({static}/images/2026/gman-glass.png)

Each ray can spawn more rays, so a pixel expands into a tree of rays. The scheme is Turner Whitted's, from 1980. Rays nest four deep, and a branch that reaches the fourth bounce gets the background color.

## One term per bounce

One equation describes all the light in a scene:

<div class="highlight"><pre><code>L = L<sub>e</sub> + K*L</code></pre></div>

L is that light, L<sub>e</sub> the light the lamps emit and K what one bounce does to light. L appears on both sides because reflected light is still light, and it reflects again. Substitute L into itself and the equation unrolls, one term per bounce:

<div class="highlight"><pre><code>L = L<sub>e</sub> + K*L<sub>e</sub> + K²*L<sub>e</sub> + ⋯</code></pre></div>

L<sub>e</sub> is light straight from a lamp, K*L<sub>e</sub> light that has bounced once, K²*L<sub>e</sub> light that has bounced twice.

## The terms it skips

A Whitted ray tracer follows light only where the direction is certain: toward a light, off a mirror and through glass. The light a matte wall passes to another matte wall scatters in every direction, and following it would take a fan of rays at every hit. The ray tracer drops those terms. The vase scene stands in for them with `LightSource "ambientlight" 1 "intensity" [0.38]`, one constant brightness added everywhere, a guess.

## Try it

Install gman with the tarball's `install.sh`, as the [README](https://github.com/jac18281828/gman#install) shows. Then, from the unpacked folder:

```sh
gman -r gmanraytracer samples/vase.rib
```

That writes `vase.png`, the ray-traced render above, in about 2.4 seconds on an Apple M3 Max. Plain `gman samples/vase.rib` renders the z-buffer preview. The [gman page](/gman.html) shows the one-line edits that turn the vase to glass and the dome to a mirror.
