## Turn it on

Three edits to `vase.rib`, the [GMAN page](/gman.html)'s sample scene: add the option ahead of `WorldBegin`, delete the `ambientlight`, and give each surface a `Ka` equal to its `Kd`.

```
Option "render" "string indirect" ["radiosity"]
```

```sh
gman -r gmanraytracer vase.rib
```

It still renders in seconds. `Option "radiosity" "float elementsize"` sets the patch size, an eighth of the scene's largest side by default.

## How it works

[John: keep, cut or reword.]

Radiosity is the two-pass method of Wallace, Cohen and Greenberg (1987), the one BMRT ran as `-radio`. The first pass dices the scene into patches and solves, by progressive refinement, how much diffuse light each patch sends every other. Light passes through a translucent surface attenuated by `1 - Os`. The second pass is the ray tracer, unchanged: it hands each shaded point its indirect light, and `ambient()` adds it to the ambient lights, so each surface weights it by its own `Ka`. Reflected and refracted hits get it too, colour bleeding included.

Transparency, shadows and reflection stay the ray tracer's. The pass only answers the question the ambient light used to fake.

## A stub since 2002

[John: the arc in your words.]

GMAN's tree has carried a `gmanradiosity` renderer since 2002, when the original project stopped. Its `render` body was empty: it built `OFF` and drew nothing. 1.0 deletes it. The pass that replaces it, `libradiosity.so`, builds by default and ships in every release download.

Getting there moved the ray tracer's geometry — the eight ray primitives, the BVH and the transmission walk — out of the ray tracer and into the shared library, where the radiosity pass and the path tracer both reach it.
