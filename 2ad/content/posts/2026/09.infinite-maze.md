Infinite Maze is a pixel-art platformer in which you climb, by torchlight,
through an Egyptian tomb that goes on forever in every direction.

Ever since I was little I have wanted to make a game with infinite
playability. I call it Infinite Maze.

The world is static, because the generator derives every room from a SHA3 hash
of the seed and the room's position. The same seed always builds the same
world, so every room is infinitely interconnected and consistent through hashing,
and every entry in the maze connects to every other entry with probability 1.
The last section says what that probability is worth.

## Climbing by torchlight

You play an explorer in a fedora who runs along corridors, climbs ladders,
walks stone stairs and jumps. The rooms are dark blue chambers carved
with hieroglyphs, and the torch lights only a circle around you, so most of
the tomb sits in shadow until you reach it.

The torch is our clock. A full torch burns for 300 seconds, and its light
shrinks as it burns, to a quarter of its reach in the last tenth. A
turquoise brazier relights it to full. An oil jar in a dead end adds 60
seconds. When the burn reaches zero the torch gutters to an ember for 10
seconds, and the run ends unless you reach a flame in that time.

![A sandstone wall fills the screen, lit gold by the torch, with a single narrow chamber cut into it. The explorer stands on a ledge beside a brass brazier burning with a turquoise flame, and a wooden ladder drops from the ledge into a dark shaft below.]({static}/images/2026/maze-brazier.png)

A brazier is your lifeline, along with oil jars. The generator spaces braziers
so that enough flame is available to solve each room, but success is not
certain.

The tomb leaves hints for a lost explorer, and it leaves more as the torch
burns down. At some junctions a carved arrow high in the room points along the
way on, and about once a tomb a skeleton slumped in a lower corner points the
same way with its arm. When the torch falls below half, a second set of
skeletons fades in, so the hints thicken when you can least afford a wrong
turn. The air helps too. In some dead ends, more often the long ones, the dust
drifts back toward the way you came in, while on the way on it hangs still, so
moving air tells you to turn around.

The music is synthesized in the browser, in the Hijaz mode on E over a frame
drum at 110 beats a minute. As the torch dims the music adds layers and its
tempo rises to 128, so you hear the torch running low before you see it.

The score counts the tombs you solve along your way on, and you solve a tomb
by leaving it through its exit door. Coins lie in dead ends and in rows of
three along corridors, and each tomb pays coins once the first time a run
solves it. Every 100 tombs a splash names a rank, from TORCHBEARER at 100 to
PHARAOH at 1,000 and PHARAOH II after that, for the patient.

## Phone, controller or keyboard

Accessibility is the key to a great experience, so the game runs in the
browser and plays on whatever you have in your hand.

- **Keyboard.** The arrows or WASD move, Space or Z jumps, P pauses and M
  mutes. Up or down takes the ladder or stair of the cell you stand in from
  anywhere on its floor.
- **Phone.** On a phone the view fills the screen. A d-pad moves you, its arms
  running, climbing and descending, and a red fire button opposite it jumps.
  Soft keys cover every screen, and PAD swaps the sides of the d-pad and the
  fire button.
- **Controller.** A standard-mapped gamepad plays with no setup once you press
  a button so the browser sees it. The d-pad or left stick moves, A jumps and
  begins, B confirms, Start pauses and resumes and Select goes to the title.

![The game on a phone held upright. The torchlit tomb fills the top of the screen with the explorer on a stone stair. Below it a round d-pad with four cream arrows sits at the left and a large red fire button at the right.]({static}/images/2026/maze-phone.png)

The touch controls and the gamepad both send the keyboard's own key events, so
the game has one set of key handlers and every input method plays the same
game.

## Save codes

You pick one of four difficulties on the title screen, and each sets how many
rooms a tomb holds: 2×2 at EASY, 3×3 at MEDIUM, 4×4 at HARD and 6×4 at ULTIMATE. The tombs
and the doors between them are the same at every level, and only the rooms
inside a tomb differ.

A save is the seed code and one integer, such as `K7Q2-M9XA:4813`. The integer
packs the signed coordinates of a room into one number:

```
z(n) = 2n for n ≥ 0, else −2n − 1
a = z(x), b = z(y)
p = a ≥ b ? a² + a + b : a + b²
```

The first line folds the signed integers onto the naturals, and the last packs
the pair into one number. Resuming inverts the pairing and rebuilds that room
straight from the hash, so the game replays nothing to get there. Since every
room is a pure function of the seed and its position, a code typed on another
device resumes in the same room.

![The pause screen over a dimmed tomb. CONTINUES 0 and TOMB 0 flank the word PAUSED at the top, a small map of the tomb fills the middle with an orange marker for the explorer, and the save code QZKA-HWDB:0 MEDIUM shows in turquoise below it, above the keys P RESUME, ARROWS PAN, Q TITLE, M MUTE and C COPY.]({static}/images/2026/maze-pause.png)

P pauses and shows the map of the passages you have walked, with the save
code under it, and C copies the code. The game also keeps your last save, its
run and your coins in local storage, so a reload asks CONTINUE? and picks up
where you stopped.

A save code holds coordinates below 2²⁵ rooms in each direction. A room is 576
pixels wide and the explorer runs at 96 pixels a second, so walking that far
sideways would take about six years of running, assuming the maze ever ran in
a straight line, which it does not.

## A world defined by static rules

A seed code is 8 Crockford base32 characters, 40 bits in all, and the
generator hashes it into a world key. Every random value in the game then
comes from one hash of that key, a tag and some coordinates:

```
WORLD = SHA3-256("infinite-maze/v1" ‖ code)
H(tag, n₁, n₂, …) = SHA3-256(WORLD ‖ tag ‖ int64be(n₁) ‖ int64be(n₂) ‖ …)
```

The integers use a fixed width, so `(1, 23)` and `(12, 3)` never share an
input. Since every value comes from a fresh hash of its own tag and
coordinates, the generator holds no chain that could cycle, and a repeated
room would need a SHA3-256 collision. This is also why the world is static. The generator keeps no state between rooms, so it can build any room
in any order, a million tombs up included, and get the same answer.

The game carries its own SHA3-256, a compact Keccak-f[1600] checked against
the FIPS 202 test vectors. Bulk randomness inside a room comes from sfc32,
seeded from the first 16 bytes of a digest. Generation uses integer arithmetic
only, so a seed builds the same world on every JavaScript engine.

The world has two scales, and both use the same rule. A board is one room, a
12×7 grid of cells, a little larger than two screens in each direction. A tomb
is a grid of boards, and tombs tile an unbounded plane.

Inside a board, the generator grows a spanning tree over the 84 cells with the
growing-tree algorithm. It extends the newest cell 60% of the time and a
random one otherwise, and it weights horizontal links 4 to 1 over vertical and
diagonal ones. A horizontal link becomes a corridor, a vertical one a ladder
and a diagonal one a stone stair. The generator then carves the tree down to
40 to 50 cells by level and leaves the rest as rock.

The game builds boards lazily. It holds the tombs within one of the explorer
in each direction and builds a board when it comes within one board of the
camera, which takes 0.7 ms on average.

## Room continuity

Each tomb leaves through one exit door, up, left or right, and never down. The
wall between two side neighbours points one way, so two neighbours never exit
into each other, and a hash of the tomb's position picks among the open
directions. A tomb holds an entry door wherever a neighbour exits into it, so
it has one exit and up to three entries.

Inside a tomb the boards form a tree that joins all its doors: a trunk from an
entry to the exit, tributaries from the other entries and dead-end branches,
with rock on every board position in none of them. Inside a board the cells form a tree that joins all of
that board's doors.

Doors match across every seam because both boards on a shared wall read the
same hash for the door's position. Neither board consults the other, so the
generator can build them in either order, or hours apart, and the doors still
line up.

## What we believe and why

Some properties hold by construction. Cell trees join into board trees through
single doors, board trees join into tomb trees, and tombs join along a flow
with no downward exit. A loop of three or more tombs would need a directed
cycle within one row, which a line cannot hold, and a loop of two would need
two doors on one wall, which holds at most one. So:

- the maze has no loops at any scale, and the path between two cells is unique;
- every cell has an unbroken route upward, because every tomb's exit walk
  climbs without end.

Connection holds with probability 1, which is weaker than certainty. Two tombs
are joined exactly when their exit walks meet. The column gap between two
walks moves like a recurrent random walk on a line, and a random walk on a
line returns to zero with probability 1 (Pólya, 1921), so the walks meet with
probability 1 and the tombs form one tree. The expected time to return is
infinite, though, so a meeting can come very late. On seed 0000-0000, the
walks from tombs (0, 0) and (10, −50) are 15 columns apart at row 0 and first
meet at row 773,851. I reran that walk for this post, and the row number held.

For any one seed the hash fixes every wall, so whether two given walks meet is
settled before anyone looks. The probability belongs to a model in which the
hash behaves like fair coin flips, and in that model the walks that never meet
have probability 0.

A test harness checks the generator from the same source the game uses, over
2,121 tombs, columns −10 to 10 by rows −50 to 50, at each of the four levels.
Way up runs once, since no difficulty changes the flow.

- **Keccak.** SHA3-256 matches the FIPS 202 test vectors.
- **Continuity.** Every door matches in position on both sides of its wall.
- **Tree.** Each tomb's cells and doors form one tree, with edges equal to
  nodes minus one, joining all of the tomb's wall doors.
- **Flow.** Each tomb exits left, right or up through exactly one door, and no
  two side neighbours exit into each other.
- **Way up.** Every tomb's exit walk climbs out of the window within 100,000
  steps.
- **Solvability.** The harness follows the exits from tomb (0, −50) to a tomb
  in row 50, and a breadth-first search over cells and doors reaches that
  tomb's exit door from the first tomb's first entry.
- **Determinism.** A fixed seed produces a fixed digest of tombs at each
  difficulty.

Apart from the walks along the generator's exits, the checks follow doors held
on both sides of a wall, so a fault in the generator's own tomb graph cannot
vouch for itself.

This is not a machine-checked proof. The construction arguments are written
out by hand in the specification, the probability-1 claim rests on a model of
the hash, and the tests cover a finite window of an infinite plane.

## One file

For all that, the game is small. It ships as one HTML file with no assets and
no network access, since the music is synthesized and the sprites live in the
source as palette strings. The source itself is modular, a pure generator in
one set of ES modules and the game in another, and a small build script
bundles them into the file the site serves.

Pick a seed and start climbing at [maze.2ad.com](https://maze.2ad.com).
